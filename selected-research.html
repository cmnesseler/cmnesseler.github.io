Selected research

Mapping discrimination in Europe through a field experiment in amateur sport

with Carlos Gomez-Gonzalez and Helmut Dietl

Using mock accounts with native- or foreign-sounding names, we emailed 23,020 amateur football clubs in 22 European countries and asked to join a training session. Response rates differed by country, and on average they were about 10% lower for foreign-sounding names.

Gender Information and Perceived Quality: An Experiment with Professional Soccer Performance

with Carlos Gomez-Gonzalez, Dave Berri and Helmut Dietl

Participants rated video clips of elite women’s and men’s football players. A control group saw the original videos, in which the players’ gender was visible; a treatment group saw the same videos with gender obscured by blurring. Men’s videos were rated higher only when participants could see that the players were men. With gender blurred, ratings for women and men did not differ.

Examining Discrimination against Jews in Italy with Three Natural Field Experiments

with Carlos Gomez-Gonzalez, Petr Parshakov and Helmut Dietl

Three natural field experiments on anti-Semitism in Italy. We emailed amateur football clubs, landlords and employers, using Jewish- and non-Jewish-sounding names, and compared the replies. We found significant discrimination when looking for a football club and an apartment, but not when looking for a job.

The three experiments: Housing market; Labor market; Social integration (football).

After the Crimea crisis: Employee discrimination in Russia and Ukraine

with Iuliia Naidenova, Petr Parshakov and Aleksei Chusovliankin

The annexation of Crimea created a political crisis in Russian–Ukrainian relations. Using a quasi-experimental design, we study whether it led to employee discrimination in the major sports leagues of both countries. Employment conditions worsened significantly after the crisis began.
