Latest

Knocking on sports clubs’ doors: A field experiment on ethnic discrimination in Germany

PNAS Nexus, June 2026. With Carlos Gomez-Gonzalez, Petr Parshakov, Thadeu Gasparetto and Helmut Dietl.

We emailed more than 8,900 amateur sports clubs in Germany, from football and handball to golf and horse riding, asking to join. Each email was signed with either a German-sounding name or a name suggesting Turkish, Syrian or Ukrainian origin. Applicants with foreign-sounding names were about 6 percentage points less likely to get a positive reply, and the gap was largest for Syrian and Turkish names.

Research

Field experiments

We send requests, such as asking to join a club, that are identical except for one detail, usually the sender’s name, and compare the replies. We have used this to measure discrimination in amateur sport across Europe and among frontline welfare workers in Norway.

Field experiment studies

Sports as a laboratory

Sports keep a precise, public record of performance, which makes bias easier to see. I study how gender and skin tone change how players are judged, how coaches and referees decide, and whether penalty-kick advice holds up on the pitch.

Sports studies

Esports as a laboratory

Online games produce large, detailed data. I have used them to study how Russia’s invasion affected Ukrainian and Russian professional players, how players responded when a game character came out as gay, and how obesity relates to performance.
