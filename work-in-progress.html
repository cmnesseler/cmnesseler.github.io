Work in progress

  • Violent Video Games and Aggression - A Meta-Analysis

    with Petr Parshakov and Iuliia Naidenova

    A meta-analysis of more than 110 peer-reviewed studies published since 2010 on violent video games and aggression. Results differ between pre-registered and non-pre-registered studies, and between experimental and non-experimental ones. Studies with null results are underrepresented, a sign of publication bias.

  • Users Favor LLM-Generated Content—Until They Know It’s AI

    with Petr Parshakov, Iuliia Naidenova, Sofia Paklina and Nikita Matkin

    In a randomized field experiment, participants rated human- and AI-written answers to popular questions, with the source either disclosed or hidden. Participants generally preferred the AI answers, but the preference shrank significantly once they knew an answer came from AI.

  • Together but not scrambled? Ethnic discrimination and a low-cost intervention

    with Carlos Gomez-Gonzalez, Petr Parshakov and Helmut Dietl

    A large-scale field experiment testing whether a short video can reduce discrimination. About 600 Swiss amateur soccer coaches received an email with a two-minute video in which two famous Swiss soccer players promote inclusivity, and clubs then received applications with Swiss- and foreign-sounding names. Overall the video had no positive effect on responses, but it did among the clubs that opened it.

  • Different perspectives. How viewers evaluate men and women athletes.

    with Sofya Kulikova, Iuliia Naidenova, Petr Parshakov and Daria Semenova

    An eye-tracking study that replicates earlier findings on how viewers evaluate men and women athletes, and extends them to esports videos.

  • How to increase sports club participation? Results from a natural field experiment.

    with Espen Sagen and Carlos Gomez-Gonzalez

    In a natural field experiment in Rogaland, Norway, elementary school students received information about sports clubs near them. We measure whether more children joined a club two months later.

  • Investigating social integration via local language acquisition

    with Hammad Shaikh and Jiaqi Zou

    Does learning the local language help non-native residents rent an apartment or join a local recreational sports team? We study this in Austria, Slovakia, Czechia and Hungary.

  • Discrimination in Online Gaming. Results from a field experiment

    with Raphael Prifti

    Players in seven online games randomly changed their names to signal different genders or nationalities. Four games showed little or no discrimination, while three showed pronounced harassment of female players. Female players signalling Chinese origin faced the most discrimination.

  • Take One for the Team. Directing and Contributing Team Performance.

    with Iuliia Naidenova, Petr Parshakov and Carlos Gomez-Gonzalez

    Using more than 300,000 player observations from Counter-Strike: Global Offensive, we find that team leaders underperform their teammates. But the more a leader sacrifices individual performance, the higher the team’s win rate.
